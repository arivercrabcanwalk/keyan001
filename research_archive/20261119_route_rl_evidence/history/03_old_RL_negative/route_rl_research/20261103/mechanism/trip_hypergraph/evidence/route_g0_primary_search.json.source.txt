## Introduction

In the contemporary logistics landscape, the rapid expansion of e-commerce has placed unprecedented pressure on last-mile delivery systems. As urbanization intensifies, traditional delivery models relying solely on heavy trucks face severe challenges, including escalating traffic congestion and stringent carbon emission regulations in metropolitan areas (Savelsbergh and Van Woensel, 2016). These ground-based limitations often result in delivery delays and increased operational costs, making it difficult to meet the growing customer demand for same-day or instant delivery services. Consequently, the integration of drones into logistics networks has emerged. For instance, Amazon’s Prime Air program aims to complete customer orders within 30 min (Pierce, 2013). Similarly, UPS Flight Forward has been delivering vaccines using drones (Wolf, 2021). 
Motivated by these gaps, this paper proposes the Heterogeneous Drone-Truck Routing Problem with En-route Launch and Recovery (HDTRP-ELR). Our model is the first to simultaneously integrate ELR, multi-visit capabilities, and heterogeneous fleet management within a single framework, together with a comprehensive energy consumption function that accounts for payload-dependent discharge dynamics. The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

  * 1)

We formulate an MISOCP model for the HDTRP-ELR, which rigorously defines the synchronization constraints required for launching and recovering heterogeneous drones on arcs rather than strictly at nodes, and incorporates a multi-visit mechanism allowing drones to serve multiple customers per sortie and a realistic energy consumption model with payload-dependent discharge rates.

  * 2)

A Reinforcement Learning-guided Decoupled Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (RLD-ALNS) algorithm is developed to solve this NP-hard problem efficiently. The algorithm embeds a Deep Q-Network (DQN) agent that dynamically selects destruction operators and intensities based on the structural features of the current solution, and employs a dual-head MLP proxy to filter unpromising candidate solutions before exact evaluation.

#### Abstract

Urban logistics face complexity due to traffic congestion, fleet heterogeneity, warehouse constraints, and driver workload balancing, especially in the Heterogeneous Multi-Trip Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows and Time-Varying Networks (HMTVRPTW-TVN). We develop a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model with dual-peak time discretization and exact linearization for heterogeneous fleet coordination. Given the NP-hard nature, we propose a Hyper-Heuristic based on Cumulative Reward Q-Learning (HHCRQL), integrating reinforcement learning with heuristic operators in a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The algorithm dynamically selects operators using a four-dimensional state space and a cumulative reward function combining timestep and fitness. Experiments show that, for small instances, HHCRQL achieves solutions within 3% of Gurobi’s optimum when customer nodes exceed 15, outperforming Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) and LNS with Simulated Annealing (LNSSA) with stable, shorter runtime. For large-scale instances, HHCRQL reduces gaps by up to 9.17% versus Iterated Local Search (ILS), 6.74% versus LNS, and 5.95% versus LNSSA, while maintaining relatively stable runtime. Real-world validation using Shanghai logistics data reduces waiting times by 35.36% and total transportation times by 24.68%, confirming HHCRQL’s effectiveness, robustness, and scalability.

## Abstract

The integration of autonomous aerial drones with traditional ground vehicle fleets offers substantial potential for multi-echelon last-mile delivery networks. However, coordinating multi-modal fleets under operational constraints presents an underlying challenge: bridging discrete routing decisions with the continuous spatio-temporal progression of physical transport. While Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) provides an adaptive paradigm for dynamic dispatching, evaluating learned policies requires simulation environments that accurately synchronize discrete allocation logic with the continuous operational state progression of logistical entities. To address this need, this paper presents a Python-native, modular simulation and reinforcement learning environment specifically formulated for coordinated truck-drone delivery systems with intermediate micro-hubs. The core contributions are threefold: (1) a hybrid modeling framework that couples discrete event-based dispatching with the continuous spatio-temporal operational simulation of logistical entities; (2) an extensible, event-driven constraint manager that dynamically enforces defined logistical feasibility, including vehicle payload limits, spatial operating boundaries, and inter-vehicle synchronization, while constraining the combinatorial action space through invalid-action masking and a structured node-pair representation; and (3) an empirical evaluation benchmarking a Maskable Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent against classical dispatch heuristics and unmasked learning configurations across varying operational scales. By offering a standardized and reproducible benchmarking platform, this work provides a practical simulation infrastructure for developing and evaluating constraint-aware reinforcement learning algorithms for operational planning in synchronized multi-modal freight logistics.
